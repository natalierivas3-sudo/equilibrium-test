Our story

Where do you go when you don't know where to look?

Natalie Rivas

Founder · MD/MPH candidate, Dell Medical School and UTHealth Houston School of Public Health · UT Austin, 2017–2021 · Future psychiatrist

LinkedIn ↗

I still remember the drive in August 2017. Hurricane Harvey was hitting the Coastal Bend as my grandmother and my dad drove me up from Beeville to a school I had never even toured.

I was overwhelmed from the moment I arrived. UT felt like a city of its own, with more students on campus than people in my hometown, and I was culture shocked and unsure of what to do with myself.

Being the first in my family to go to college added another layer. There was no one at home who could tell me how any of it worked, like where to go when I was struggling or that it was okay to need a break. So much of what helps you settle into a new place is knowledge that gets passed down, and no one had been able to pass it down to me yet.

When I look back on my years in Austin, I often think about that 18 year old and what would have made that first year easier. She didn’t need more pamphlets. She needed someone to show her a quiet place to study, a park to sit in, where to meet people, and who to talk to, and to remind her that she belonged in every one of those places.

That is who I made The Equilibrium for. It’s the guide I wish I had found back then, both in Austin and at UT and even during my time at Dell Med.

Why this matters to me

I believe that mental health care doesn’t begin in a clinic, but in the places and people that keep us steady long before things get hard. My public health training has only made that clearer. Where we live and spend our time shapes how we feel, so if I can make those places easier to find, especially for students who are new, far from home, or the first in their families like me, I believe that counts as care too.

What The Equilibrium is

The Equilibrium brings together free and low-cost ways to rest, move, get outside, study, connect, and find support, around UT and across Austin. It's for students, newcomers, and anyone who could use a little more balance in their day.

Built by students, for students still finding their people and their places. It started as a project with the Dell Med Psychiatry Interest Group. Students pick the spots, and your feedback decides what gets added next.

What it is, and what it isn't

This is a student-maintained guide, not a substitute for professional or emergency care. If you need help right now, crisis support is available 24/7.
